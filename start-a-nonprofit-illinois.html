Illinois nonprofit guide

How to start a nonprofit in Illinois

Forming an Illinois nonprofit is not one filing. It is a sequence: organize the corporation with the state, adopt governance documents, obtain an EIN, seek federal tax exemption, and complete any Illinois charity-registration requirements.

Illinois snapshot: The Secretary of State lists a $50 filing fee for Form NFP 102.10. Its online instructions require at least three directors and an incorporator who is a natural person age 18 or older.

The Illinois nonprofit formation sequence

  1. Define the charitable purpose. Write what the organization will do, whom it will serve, and where it will operate.
  2. Select the initial board. Illinois online filing instructions require at least three directors. Decide who will serve as president or chair, secretary, and treasurer.
  3. Check the proposed name. Confirm that it is distinguishable in the Illinois business-entity database before printing materials or opening accounts.
  4. Choose a registered agent and Illinois office. Use an eligible physical Illinois address where official notices can be received.
  5. Prepare and file Form NFP 102.10. Include purpose language appropriate for the intended exempt status. State incorporation alone does not create federal 501(c)(3) recognition.
  6. Adopt bylaws and governance policies. Hold the organizational meeting, approve bylaws, elect officers, authorize a bank account, and preserve signed minutes.
  7. Obtain an EIN. Request the organization’s federal employer identification number directly from the IRS.
  8. Apply for federal recognition. Use Form 1023 or, if eligible after completing the IRS worksheet, Form 1023-EZ.
  9. Address Illinois charity registration. The Attorney General states that charitable organizations soliciting donations or holding charitable assets in Illinois must register.
  10. Create an annual compliance calendar. Track the Illinois annual report, IRS Form 990-series return, charity renewals, meetings, minutes, and conflict disclosures.

Common Illinois mistakes

  • Assuming state incorporation automatically creates federal tax exemption.
  • Using a vague purpose statement that does not support the intended charitable classification.
  • Failing to keep bylaws and signed board minutes because they are not sent with the state filing.
  • Soliciting contributions before addressing Illinois Attorney General registration.
  • Missing the annual report or IRS information-return deadline after formation.

Frequently asked questions

How much does Illinois charge to incorporate a nonprofit?

The Illinois Secretary of State currently lists a $50 filing fee for Form NFP 102.10, with separate expedited and payment-processing charges potentially applicable.

How many directors does an Illinois nonprofit need?

The Illinois Secretary of State’s online nonprofit-incorporation instructions require at least three directors and allow three to seven directors to be listed online.

Does filing Articles make us a 501(c)(3)?

No. Illinois incorporation creates the state-law entity. Federal recognition generally requires a separate Form 1023-series application to the IRS.

Do Illinois charities register before fundraising?

The Illinois Attorney General states that charitable organizations that solicit donations or hold charitable assets in Illinois must register with its office. Review the current instructions for timing and exceptions.
