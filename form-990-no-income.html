Annual IRS filing

Does a nonprofit file Form 990 if it had no income?

Usually, yes. No income does not normally erase the annual IRS reporting requirement. Many eligible small organizations can submit the short electronic Form 990-N instead of a full Form 990.

The key distinction: Form 990 is an information-reporting system, not simply an income-tax bill. An organization may have nothing to report as revenue and still need to submit an annual return or notice.

What a small organization usually files

The IRS says most small tax-exempt organizations with an annual reporting requirement may use Form 990-N, the electronic e-Postcard, when gross receipts are normally $50,000 or less. Zero receipts are below that threshold, but the organization must still pass the complete eligibility rules.

Gross receipts means everything received from all sources during the accounting period before subtracting expenses. It is not the same as profit or the year-end bank balance.

Do not assume “no activity” means “no filing”

Having no donations, grants, sales, payroll, or expenses does not itself create an annual-filing exception. The IRS identifies exceptions based mainly on organization type and status. Churches, certain church-affiliated organizations, and some other categories can be treated differently.

Likewise, an organization that has not yet received its IRS determination letter may still have a filing obligation. See our separate guide to filing while a 501(c)(3) application is pending.

When the filing is due

The due date is generally the 15th day of the fifth month after the tax year ends. A calendar-year organization ending December 31 normally files by May 15 of the following year. If that date is a weekend or legal holiday, the due date moves to the next business day.

Form 990-N cannot be filed until the tax year has ended, and the IRS does not offer an extension for the e-Postcard. Other Form 990-series returns may have separate extension procedures.

A simple first-year process

  1. Confirm the tax year. Identify the month when the organization's annual accounting period ends.
  2. Total gross receipts. Count amounts received before subtracting any expenses, even if the organization ended the year with no money.
  3. Check the filing exception list. Do not rely on a verbal assumption that the organization is exempt from reporting.
  4. Check Form 990-N eligibility. Review the current IRS rules for the organization's age, receipts, and organization type.
  5. File through the IRS system. Save the electronic acceptance and a record of the information submitted.
  6. Track the next deadline. Add the annual IRS filing and separate state reports to the compliance calendar.
Useful records even in a zero-income year: Keep bank statements, donation records, receipts, board minutes, the EIN letter, formation documents, and the Form 990-N acceptance. A quiet year still needs a clean paper trail.

Why the deadline matters

The IRS does not assess a late-filing penalty for Form 990-N, but it says the organization should submit the notice even if late. Missing a required Form 990, 990-EZ, 990-PF, or 990-N for three consecutive years causes automatic revocation of federal tax-exempt status on the third filing due date.

Frequently asked questions

Does a nonprofit file Form 990 if it had no income?

Usually it still has an annual IRS reporting requirement. Most eligible small tax-exempt organizations whose gross receipts are normally $50,000 or less can satisfy that requirement with Form 990-N, even if receipts were zero.

When is Form 990-N due?

It is due on the 15th day of the fifth month after the tax year ends. For a calendar-year organization, the normal due date is May 15 of the following year.

Can every nonprofit with no income file Form 990-N?

No. Some organizations are exempt from annual filing, while others cannot use Form 990-N and must file another Form 990-series return. Check the current IRS instructions for the organization's type and circumstances.

What happens after three missed annual filings?

An organization that fails to file a required Form 990-series return or notice for three consecutive years automatically loses its federal tax-exempt status.
