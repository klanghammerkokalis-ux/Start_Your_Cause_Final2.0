The Wisconsin path, in plain language

1
Define your charitable purpose and organization.
Clarify your mission, intended activities, and who the organization will serve.
2
Choose your initial leadership and governance structure.
Decide how the nonprofit will be governed and who will serve in its initial leadership roles.
3
Choose a name, registered agent, and Wisconsin registered office.
Wisconsin's nonstock corporation filing asks for the entity name, principal office, registered office, registered agent, and incorporator information.
4
File Wisconsin nonstock Articles of Incorporation.
Wisconsin DFI Form 102 creates a domestic nonstock corporation. The current online filing fee published by DFI is $35. Organizations seeking 501(c)(3) recognition should ensure their organizing document contains appropriate federal tax-exemption language.
5
Adopt bylaws and document organizational action.
Create operating rules, approve key organizational decisions, and preserve appropriate records.
6
Get an EIN and determine your IRS application path.
Organizations seeking federal recognition generally use Form 1023 or, when eligible, Form 1023-EZ. Eligibility for 1023-EZ depends on the IRS Eligibility Worksheet.
7
Check Wisconsin charitable-registration requirements.
Wisconsin generally requires registration when a charitable organization solicits in the state and has paid employees and/or receives at least $25,000 in contributions during a fiscal year, subject to exemptions. Organizations that must register currently use Wisconsin DFI Form 296.
8
Plan for ongoing reporting.
Registered Wisconsin charitable organizations have annual renewal and financial-reporting requirements. Your exact obligations depend on your organization's activities and finances.
