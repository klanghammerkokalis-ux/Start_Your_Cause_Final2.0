Board planning

Nonprofit board requirements for new organizations

Board requirements come from state law, the Articles, the bylaws, and federal tax-exemption expectations. The board must be real: it governs the organization rather than merely lending names to a founder.

Core decisions for a new board

  • Minimum and maximum number of directors.
  • Director qualifications and independence.
  • Initial and future terms.
  • Election, resignation, removal, and vacancy procedures.
  • Officer positions and separation of duties.
  • Meeting notice, quorum, voting, and remote participation.
  • Conflict disclosure and recusal.
  • Financial approval thresholds and recordkeeping.

Illinois starting point

The Illinois Secretary of State’s online Articles instructions require at least three directors and allow three to seven directors to be listed in that filing flow. The bylaws should establish a workable structure consistent with the Articles and current law.

A practical founding board

A three-person board might assign chair or president, secretary, and treasurer responsibilities, but titles alone are not enough. Directors should understand the mission, review finances, participate in meetings, ask questions, disclose conflicts, and preserve minutes.

Independence and family relationships

A board dominated by one household or people financially dependent on the founder can create credibility and conflict concerns even when not categorically prohibited. Grantmakers, banks, regulators, and the IRS may examine whether decisions protect the organization rather than private interests.

Board test: If the founder disagrees with the rest of the board, can the board still make and enforce a legitimate decision? If not, the governance structure may exist only on paper.

Frequently asked questions

How many board members does a nonprofit need?

The minimum depends on state law and the governing documents. Illinois online nonprofit-incorporation instructions require at least three directors.

Can family members serve together?

Family service may be allowed, but related-party control creates conflict, independence, and credibility concerns. Review state law, IRS expectations, and funder requirements.

Can the founder be board president?

Often yes, subject to state law and the governing documents. The board must still exercise independent oversight and manage conflicts.

Do directors have to be paid?

No. Many startup nonprofit directors serve without compensation. Reimbursement and compensation policies should be reasonable, approved, and documented.
