IRS application guide

Can a nonprofit accept donations while 501(c)(3) is pending?

Yes—with careful communication. An organization can receive contributions before its IRS determination arrives, but donors do not have advance assurance that those gifts are tax deductible.

The practical answer: You may receive and track contributions while Form 1023 or Form 1023-EZ is pending. Tell donors the application is pending and avoid promising deductibility before the IRS decides.

What the IRS says

The IRS says contributors have no advance assurance of deductibility while an exemption application is pending. If the organization is ultimately recognized as exempt for the period when a contribution was made, the contribution will be deductible. If the organization does not qualify for that period, it will not be deductible.

This distinction matters: accepting money and guaranteeing a donor’s tax deduction are not the same thing.

Why the 27-month window matters

Generally, when an organization files Form 1023 or Form 1023-EZ within 27 months after the end of the month in which it was legally formed—and the IRS approves the application—its exemption is effective from its legal formation date. Filing later can make the submission date the effective date unless an exception applies.

Do not treat approval as automatic. Filing an application does not guarantee that the IRS will recognize exemption, and the determination letter controls the organization’s effective date and classification.

A cautious founder checklist

  1. Confirm the entity is legally formed. Keep accepted formation documents, bylaws, board actions, and the EIN notice in the permanent record book.
  2. Submit the correct IRS application. Use the current official eligibility worksheet before choosing Form 1023-EZ instead of Form 1023.
  3. Describe the status accurately. Say “Our application for recognition under Section 501(c)(3) is pending.” Do not say the organization is IRS-recognized if it is not.
  4. Avoid guaranteeing deductibility. Explain that deductibility depends on the IRS’s final determination and effective date, and donors should consult their own tax advisers.
  5. Track every gift. Record the donor, date, amount, restrictions, payment method, acknowledgment, and any goods or services provided in return.
  6. Check fundraising registration rules. State charitable-solicitation requirements are separate from federal exemption and may apply before asking the public for donations.
  7. Calendar annual filings now. Unless an exception applies, Form 990-series or Form 990-N obligations begin when the organization is formed, even while the IRS application is pending.

Plain-language donor wording

“Our application for recognition of exemption under Section 501(c)(3) is pending with the IRS. Contributions may become tax deductible if the IRS recognizes our exemption for the period in which your gift was made. We cannot guarantee deductibility; please consult your tax adviser.”

This is educational sample language, not a substitute for advice tailored to the organization, fundraising campaign, or donor.

Do not forget donor acknowledgments

A pending application does not eliminate good recordkeeping. Provide prompt written acknowledgments and follow the current IRS substantiation and disclosure rules, including special requirements for contributions of $250 or more and quid pro quo contributions.

Frequently asked questions

Can a nonprofit accept donations while Form 1023 is pending?

An organization can receive contributions while its application is pending, but the IRS says donors do not have advance assurance that their contributions are deductible.

Are pending-period donations tax deductible?

They are deductible if the IRS ultimately recognizes exemption for the period when the gift was made. If the organization does not qualify for that period, the contribution is not deductible.

Why does the 27-month filing window matter?

Generally, if an eligible organization files within 27 months after the end of its formation month and the IRS approves the application, exemption is effective from the legal formation date.

Does a pending application delay Form 990 filing?

No. Unless an exception applies, annual return or notice obligations begin when the organization is formed, even while the exemption application is pending.
