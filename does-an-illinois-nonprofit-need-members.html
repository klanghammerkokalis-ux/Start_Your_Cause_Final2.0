Illinois governance

Does an Illinois nonprofit need members?

No. Illinois nonprofit corporations may have voting members or may have no members. The choice affects who holds governance authority, so founders should make it deliberately and describe it consistently in the articles or bylaws.

Plan your nonprofit bylaws →
The Illinois rule: Section 107.03 says a nonprofit corporation may have one or more classes of members or no members. If there will be no members, that fact must appear in the articles of incorporation or bylaws. If there are member classes, their qualifications and rights must be described in the articles or bylaws.

What “member” means in this context

A legal corporate member is not simply anyone who supports the mission. Members may receive governance rights under the articles, bylaws, and state law—such as electing directors or approving certain major decisions.

That is different from:

  • Directors: people serving on the governing board.
  • Donors: people or organizations making contributions.
  • Volunteers: people contributing time or services.
  • Program participants: people receiving or participating in services.
  • Marketing members: subscribers, friends, patrons, or supporters given that label without corporate voting rights.

Using “member” casually in a website, application, dues program, or handbook can create confusion. If supporters are not legal voting members, say that clearly and avoid promising governance rights the bylaws do not provide.

Member corporation versus memberless corporation

No voting membersThe board carries the governance authority assigned by law and the governing documents and generally selects successor directors as the bylaws provide.Many founder-led public charities, service organizations, foundations, and organizations that want board-centered governance.

Neither structure is automatically better or more charitable. The right choice depends on who should have enforceable governance rights and how the organization expects to operate over time.

A memberless nonprofit still needs directors

Illinois requires every nonprofit corporation to have a board with at least three directors. Directors do not have to be corporation members unless the articles or bylaws impose that qualification. Choosing no members therefore does not allow one founder to govern without a board.

For a corporation with no voting members on a matter, Illinois law generally treats requirements for member notice, presence, voting, consent, or other action as satisfied by the corresponding action of the directors. The articles, bylaws, and specific statute still need to be reviewed for the decision at hand.

If the nonprofit will have voting members

The articles or bylaws should clearly define:

  • Each membership class and who qualifies.
  • How a person or entity becomes, renews, resigns, or loses membership.
  • Whether dues are required and what happens if they are unpaid.
  • Which classes can vote and how many votes each member has.
  • How members receive notice, attend meetings, establish quorum, and vote.
  • Whether members elect directors or approve amendments, mergers, dissolution, or other major actions.
  • How membership records are maintained.

Do not create a nominal member structure merely to make the organization sound participatory. Governance rights should be workable, understood, and followed in practice.

If the nonprofit will have no members

  1. Say so expressly. Put the no-member provision in the articles or bylaws.
  2. Define board succession. State how directors are nominated, elected, removed, and replaced.
  3. Set governance safeguards. Address terms, quorum, officers, committees, meetings, and conflicts.
  4. Use supporter language carefully. A “membership program” for newsletters or benefits should say it does not create corporate voting rights if that is true.
  5. Keep records consistent. Articles, bylaws, minutes, IRS filings, public descriptions, and actual practice should not contradict one another.
Practical drafting point: “The corporation shall have no members” is a governance statement. It does not mean the nonprofit cannot have donors, volunteers, clients, subscribers, patrons, or community advisory groups.

What the IRS asks

The current Form 1023 instructions tell applicants to enter zero if they have no members. If they do have members, the IRS asks how membership is obtained and requests the rights and benefits associated with each level.

Form 990 governance reporting also focuses on substance rather than labels. Its instructions treat a person as a member for governance purposes when the governing documents or applicable law give that person governance or certain financial rights. The form separately asks whether anyone outside the governing body can elect or appoint directors or approve board decisions.

Accurately describing governance helps the IRS understand where authority is vested. It also reduces the risk that bylaws promise one structure while the board operates another.

Questions to answer before choosing

  • Should a defined constituency have the legal power to elect or remove directors?
  • Will the organization realistically maintain membership lists, notices, meetings, quorum, and votes?
  • Could member approval make mission-critical decisions more representative—or unnecessarily difficult?
  • Does a parent, affiliate, denomination, chapter system, or national organization require a particular structure?
  • Are “members” receiving only program or donor benefits, or true governance rights?

Frequently asked questions

Does an Illinois nonprofit corporation need members?

No. Section 107.03 of the Illinois General Not For Profit Corporation Act says a corporation may have one or more classes of members or may have no members. If it has no members, that fact must be stated in the articles of incorporation or bylaws.

Is a nonprofit member the same as a board member?

Not necessarily. Directors serve on the governing board. Legal members may have rights defined by law and the governing documents, such as electing directors or approving significant actions. A director does not have to be a corporation member in Illinois unless the articles or bylaws require it.

Are donors and volunteers automatically members?

No. Calling someone a supporter, donor, volunteer, subscriber, or program member does not automatically give that person corporate governance rights. The articles, bylaws, membership rules, and applicable law determine legal membership rights.

Does a memberless Illinois nonprofit still need a board?

Yes. Illinois nonprofit corporations must have a board of at least three directors. When there are no voting members for a matter, the Act generally places actions otherwise requiring member participation with the directors, subject to the articles, bylaws, and applicable law.
