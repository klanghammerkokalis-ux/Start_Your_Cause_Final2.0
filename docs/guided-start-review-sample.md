This fictional example shows the proposed scope of the $149 administrative planning offer. The scope confirmation is a draft for review. Fill in the approved business terms before requesting payment.

## Sample roadmap

Example founder in Illinois, with no formation paperwork submitted.

1. **Organize your records.** Keep your mission draft, the names and contact details of your planning team, and a list of decisions you still need to make in one place.
2. **Read the public filing instructions.** Use the [IRS state links directory](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/state-links) to find the relevant Illinois offices. Record each agency, form name, fee, and date checked. Keep separate entries for incorporation, fundraising registration, and tax matters.
3. **Prepare questions for a professional consultation.** Bring your draft mission and planned activities. Ask which governance choices and filing obligations apply to your organization, and what a document review would cost.
4. **Keep a decision and filing log.** Track the date, source, and person responsible for each item. Keep copies of anything you submit and any acknowledgments you receive.

## Sample follow-up

**Founder question.** Can you tell me whether our activities qualify for 501(c)(3) and write the purpose clause for our articles?

**Response.** Those questions need advice about your organization’s specific facts. A nonprofit attorney or qualified tax professional can help evaluate eligibility, and a nonprofit attorney can advise on the clause. For that conversation, gather your mission draft, a description of planned activities, and the most recent agency instructions. I can help you locate the records and public-resource sections in your roadmap.

## Items for counsel to review

- Whether the intake, sample roadmap, and follow-up keep the work within administrative organization and education in the jurisdictions served.
- Whether the operator’s actual correspondence, document generation, and marketing follow those boundaries.
- The approved cancellation, missed-delivery, and refund terms before they are used with customers.
